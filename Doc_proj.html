Netflix Visualization Project

Design Process

I started by exploring the Netflix Titles dataset, which contains metadata about over 8,000 titles including movies and TV shows. I brainstormed questions that would be interesting to explore, such as: When was the most content released? Which genres are most popular in which countries? How do durations vary over time?

I sketched initial layout ideas on paper to help decide the layout and structure of the final interface. The project evolved through multiple iterations where I adjusted axes, filtered invalid data, refined tooltips, and tested different chart types.

Design Choices & Rationale

  • Scatterplot (Release Year vs Duration): This chart visualizes how the length of shows has changed over time. I encoded type (Movie or TV Show) using color and cast size (as a popularity proxy) using circle size.
  • Bar Chart (Genre vs Country): To explore global trends, I broke down the top combinations of genre and country. This helps reveal regional preferences in Netflix content.
  • Line Chart (Releases Over Time): This clearly shows how Netflix’s content library has grown over the years, peaking in certain years.
  • Dropdown Filters: Users can filter content by type (Movie/TV) and rating (G, PG, etc.) to focus on specific subsets of data.

Insights & Discoveries

  • Netflix dramatically expanded content releases around 2017–2019.
  • TV Shows often have shorter durations (1 Season = 60 minutes assumed), while movies vary widely in length.
  • Genres like “Drama” and “International Movies” appear across many countries, especially the United States, India, and the UK.
  • The largest casts appear in Bollywood or action films, which boosts circle size in the scatterplot.
